An Open Source Program Office (OSPO) is an organizational framework designed to drive the strategic utilization of and healthy contribution to open source software (OSS). Organizational structures and focus areas vary across enterprises; at [Company Name], the OSPO functions as **[Company OSPO Positioning and Priority Areas]**.

The primary role of the OSPO is establishing **policy formulation**, **guideline maintenance**, and **governance frameworks** for internal open source activities, overseeing:

- Formulating open source usage policies and license guidelines
- Establishing compliance management frameworks in coordination with stakeholder departments
- Facilitating engagement with internal and external open source communities
- Monitoring technology trends and providing strategic recommendations to executive management
- Building mechanisms empowering employee contributions to OSS
- [Company-Specific OSPO Initiatives]

At [Company Name], the OSPO operates as a **[Operating Model (e.g., Virtual Team (V-Team) Model)]** led by **[Lead Department Name]**, bringing together members from **[Partner Dept 1 (e.g., Legal)]**, **[Partner Dept 2 (e.g., Security)]**, and **[Partner Dept 3 (e.g., Engineering)]**.

The OSPO is **not a helpdesk performing OSS operational tasks on behalf of teams**; rather, it designs policies, rules, and workflows that empower the entire organization to manage OSS autonomously.
(Hence the title **"Open Source Program Office"** rather than "Open Source Support Office.")

■ OSPO Organizational Models

In enterprises where open source is not the primary commercial product, standalone full-time OSPO departments are rare.
Most organizations adopt virtual OSPO teams—where cross-functional staff from R&D, Legal, Compliance, and Security drive open source governance as part of their responsibilities.

Emphasize that the OSPO's role is "building frameworks for autonomous decision-making," avoiding becoming a proxy operational bottleneck. While initial responsibilities often concentrate on key individuals, evolve toward distributed responsibilities as operations mature.

■ Managing Contact Information

Use group mailing lists rather than individual personal emails, reviewing rosters periodically.
Ensure internal portals provide FAQs and OSS request forms. Keep channels open for all employees to join.

■ Focus Initiatives

Align OSPO initiatives with overall corporate strategic roadmaps.
Under resource constraints, prioritize high-impact initiatives before expanding scope.

KPI Examples (by organizational maturity):
 * Early stage: License management coverage rate, training session counts.
 * Advanced stage: Developer autonomous review rate, reduction in OSPO consultation ticket volume.
 * Mature stage: Upstream contribution counts, company-launched open source project impact metrics.

■ External Community Engagement

The OSPO role extends beyond internal governance to encompass community relationship building.
Consider including these external focus areas:
 * Formulating upstream contribution guidelines for critical OSS dependencies.
 * Encouraging employee speaking and participation at open source events.
 * Establishing channels for communicating corporate open source strategy externally.
 * Creating supportive policies for community participation during working hours.

■ Expert Roster Maintenance

Ensure group mailing lists are maintained so inquiries receive timely responses even during staff transitions.
OSPO leads should sync regularly with expert stakeholders to resolve emerging open source challenges.
