# Article 8. Employees' Personal OSS Activities

## Sample Article

Article 8. Employees' Personal OSS Activities

1. When an Employee develops, releases, or contributes to OSS projects in a personal capacity not directly related to Company duties, the Employee shall not use Company copyrighted works or internal information, nor violate internal regulations. Furthermore, Employees shall not use the Company name and must take sufficient care to ensure such actions are not mistaken for official Company business.

## Commentary

Personal OSS activity benefits companies through skill development and community networking, but requires boundaries to prevent leakage of confidential assets, corporate credit, or company resources. Open source communities disseminate information quickly, making recovery after a leak difficult. Confidentiality obligations remain legally binding during personal time; departure or career transition periods carry heightened risks of unintentional leakage. Using company equipment or work hours creates labor, cost, and IP ownership issues, while unauthorized use of corporate names/trademarks risks misrepresentation as official views. Balanced rules guide safe engagement rather than total prohibition.

## Customization Perspectives and Operational Notes

Adopt a "Permitted in principle + Narrowly defined prohibitions" structure to prevent unnecessary risk aversion.

- Keep restrictions to a minimal necessary scope. Provide sample disclaimer phrasing (e.g., "Views expressed are personal and do not represent my employer") in the OSS Operational Guidelines so employees can participate with confidence.
- Allowing personal activities during working hours depends on employment regulations and corporate culture. Verify alignment with non-compete clauses and limit restrictions strictly to necessary cases.
