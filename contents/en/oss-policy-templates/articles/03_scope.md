# Article 3. Scope of Application

## Sample Article

Article 3. Scope of Application

1. This Policy applies to the following activities conducted in connection with Company business by officers, employees, and all individuals engaged in business under the Company's control (hereinafter referred to as "Employees"):
  a. Activities regarding the acquisition, use, modification, embedding, and redistribution of OSS
  b. Activities regarding the planning, development, release, maintenance, and termination of new OSS
  [c. Contributions to OSS Projects]
2. Specific procedures, division of responsibilities, and approval requirements for each activity in the preceding paragraph shall be separately defined in the "OSS Operational Guidelines."
3. The department (or Employee) supervising the activities in Paragraph 1 shall establish necessary contractual conditions when engaging contractors or joint development partners to ensure compliance with this Policy.

## Commentary

Ambiguity around who must comply and what must be guarded creates dual risks: non-compliance or field paralysis. OSS encompasses a broad spectrum—acquisition, use, modification, redistribution, and contribution—and omitting any phase from the lifecycle scope creates gaps. Redistribution is where licensing obligations become strictest, making explicit inclusion in the scope essential. Contractors, dispatched staff, and partner companies are frequently involved, requiring coverage of "all individuals engaged in business under control." Even for personal activities, using company names or resources impacts corporate trust, necessitating governance. Separating specific procedures into the OSS Operational Guidelines balances policy stability with operational flexibility.

## Customization Perspectives and Operational Notes

Clarifying scope is the starting point for effective execution; clearly specify "covered persons, covered actions, and exclusions."

- Define the extent of "all individuals engaged in business under control" (dispatched employees, contractors, interns, etc.) and applicability to group companies according to your corporate governance structure; phased rollout is also viable.
- Define application to contractors in a concrete, actionable manner through contractual terms (SBOM submission, NDA, etc.).
- Decide within your regulatory hierarchy whether sponsorship or event participation belongs under the OSS Policy or Public Relations/Marketing rules. If included in this Policy, explicitly define conditions and responsible parties.
