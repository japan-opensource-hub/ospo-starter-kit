# Article 10. Response to License Violations and Incidents

## Sample Article

Article 10. Response to License Violations and Incidents

1. When an Employee discovers a suspected license violation or other incident (including inquiries from external parties), the Employee shall promptly report it to the OSPO.
2. Upon receiving the report under the preceding paragraph, the OSPO shall collaborate with relevant departments to investigate facts and promptly implement necessary corrective measures.

## Commentary

Delays in incident response compound legal liabilities and reputational damage, making clear initial response procedures vital. If reporting channels are ambiguous, issues risk being ignored; establishing a mandatory duty to report even at the "suspected stage" via defined channels is critical. OSPO and Legal guidance enables selection of appropriate remediation (halt distribution, refactor, contact copyright holders), while swift escalation to executive management ensures proper customer communication. Documenting incidents and corrective actions forms the foundation for preventing recurrence and building institutional knowledge.

## Customization Perspectives and Operational Notes

Integrating this workflow into existing security/quality incident response systems unifies contact channels, lowering operational costs and improving procedural effectiveness.

- Explicitly define reporting targets and channels (dedicated email, ticketing system) to reduce reporting barriers. Detail concrete examples of suspected violations (e.g., missing copyright notices) in the OSS Operational Guidelines.
- Reflect contractual notice obligations and deadlines for customer-facing deliverables in response workflows.
- Provide post-incident review templates (root cause analysis, remediation, training) to avoid building workflows from scratch. Link with Article 12 reporting to foster organizational learning.
