# Article 2. Definitions

## Sample Article

Article 2. Definitions

The principal terms used in this Policy are defined as follows:
1. "OSS" refers to software whose source code is made public and whose use, modification, and distribution are permitted under licensing terms.
2. "OSPO" is an abbreviation for Open Source Program Office, referring to the organization prescribed in Article 4.
3. "OSS Project" refers to activities involving the development, maintenance, and management of OSS. Operations are conducted by individuals, organizations, or communities.
4. "Contribution" refers to the act of providing source code, documentation, or other deliverables to an OSS Project.
[5. "CLA" is an abbreviation for Contributor License Agreement, referring to a contract defining rights ownership and licensing conditions for deliverables submitted through contributions.]
6. "Third-Party Works" refers to copyrighted works owned by parties other than the Company.

## Commentary

Discrepancies in term interpretation cause incidents and stall decision-making, making a shared understanding essential. Enabling Legal, Security, and Engineering to use the same terminology with identical meanings accelerates evaluation. Distinguishing between "Company OSS" and "External OSS" is crucial for organizing proprietary rights to protect versus third-party rights to obey. Defining rights-processing concepts like CLA prevents inadvertent signatures and erroneous operations. Defining OSPO here clarifies the subject of governance provisions from Article 4 onward, serving as a baseline for internal training, FAQs, and checklists.

## Customization Perspectives and Operational Notes

If definitions become overly legalistic or technical, teams will stop reading them; strike a balance between clarity and accuracy.

- If an OSPO has not been established, rewrite it to "Responsible Department/Committee" to emphasize roles over nomenclature.
- Determine the scope of Contribution (code only, or including documentation, translation, and testing) by balancing procedural overhead against management comprehensiveness.
- Recommend clarifying the distinction between CLA and DCO (Developer Certificate of Origin) in supplementary notes within the OSS Operational Guidelines.
- You may add terms tailored to your deliverables, but avoid bloating the definitions—prioritize terms directly linked to licensing violations and major issues.
- Annotating cross-references to synonymous terms used in existing company regulations is helpful.
